A smaller reset that comes long before the last rite. The player chooses when the season turns. Nothing forces it.

What goes: the flowers, the beds that were cleared, the blooms in hand, and the garden upgrades (gloves, watering can, compost and their kind). The garden is bare earth again with daisies in it.

What stays: the gnomes, all of them, where they stand. The hole and how deep it is. The pebbles and the whispers. The rites. The diary and everything the story has done. Seeds that the hole has opened can be bought again at once.

What is kept is bulbs. A season pays bulbs by how many flowers were grown in it, and each next bulb asks for more than the last, so waiting longer pays more but slower: turn now for a few, or carry on for more. Each bulb makes flowers grow a little faster in every season to come, a few parts in a hundred each, to be tuned by playing. Bulbs belong to this owner; the last rite does not hand them on, only the red hats.

The choice is offered once the season is worth at least one bulb. In the first season that should be around the first roses. The button says how many bulbs turning now would pay.

Each turn moves on to the next season: spring, summer, autumn, winter, and round again. A new game starts in spring. For now a season only changes how the garden looks: the light, the lawn, the trees, the cottage. The rules and the numbers are the same in every season.

A new season is a breather. Peace of mind springs back up for a moment, a fresh morning, and within a few minutes it sinks again to where the story had left it. It is not given back for good: only the story takes it away, and the story does not forget.

The diary notes each turn of the season in a line, cosy at first and less so later.

The last rite stays the second, bigger layer: it ends the owner and hands on the red hats.
